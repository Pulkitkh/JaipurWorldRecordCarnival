03

The records, and what the claims mean

The figures are stated as they were recorded on the day. Certification, where a record has it, came from a record body — and that body's decision is the one that counts, not ours.

This site describes record attempts and the figures they reached, as those figures were counted and recorded at the time. Several have been certified by record-keeping organisations — Guinness World Records, the Limca Book of Records and the India Book of Records among them. Certification is theirs to give: each organisation sets its own criteria and applies them on the day, and where a record carries a certificate, that certificate is the authority for it, not this website.

The pages here do not set out, record by record, which body certified which attempt or on what date. If you need that for a report, an application or a piece of journalism, ask — the certificates exist and can be shown. Please do not infer from a record's presence on this site that any particular organisation has certified it.

Records are, by their nature, superseded. A figure described here as a world record was one when it was set and when this page was last revised. We do not monitor other people's attempts, and nothing on this site should be read as a claim that a record still stands today.

Photographs on this site are of the events they are captioned as. Where a page uses a drawing rather than a photograph, it is a drawing — an illustration of what an object is, made because no usable photograph exists — and it is not offered as a picture of the object.

If you believe something here is wrong, please say so. Corrections are made and the page's date is moved.

04

Sending an enquiry

Sending the form starts a conversation. It does not book an event, register you for anything, or oblige either of us to go ahead.

The form on the Take part page is an expression of interest. When you send it you get a reference and, in the ordinary course, a phone call. That is the whole of what it does. In particular:

  • It is not a booking. No date, venue or attempt is reserved by sending it, and none is held for you.
  • It does not oblige us to proceed. Not every attempt that is proposed can be attempted; capacity, timing and the rules of the record body all decide that, and sometimes the answer is no.
  • It does not oblige you to proceed. You can say no at any point, for any reason or none, and nothing is owed.
  • It is free. There is no charge for enquiring and no charge for the conversation that follows.

You agree that what you send is true — your own name, a number that reaches you, and an honest account of what you are proposing. An enquiry sent in somebody else's name wastes a phone call that a real school was waiting for.

If you use the WhatsApp button instead, your message goes to WhatsApp and reaches us there. It is the same enquiry with the same standing; only the road it travels is different. See the privacy notice for what that means.

05

If an attempt goes ahead

A real attempt is arranged in writing, separately. These terms are about the website; they are not the agreement for an event.

Where a conversation turns into an actual attempt, the arrangements — dates, venue, responsibilities, safety, insurance, photography, consent for participating children, and what each side is providing — are agreed separately and in writing at that point. Those arrangements govern the event. Nothing on this website forms part of them, and nothing here should be relied on as though it did.

Record attempts happen in real places with real crowds. Responsibility for the venue, for permissions, for the safety of the people you bring and for compliance with local requirements is settled in that written agreement, before anybody gathers.

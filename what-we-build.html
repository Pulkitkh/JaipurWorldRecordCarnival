Six stages. We are beside you for every one of them — and we tell you the truth at the first one.

The six stages Typically 3–9 months
1

The Spark

You arrive with an idea, or with nothing but a wish to do something large. We research whether a category already exists, what the current mark is, and whether a new category can be proposed. Then we tell you honestly whether it is achievable with the people and the budget you actually have.

You leave with
  • Category research and the current benchmark
  • An honest feasibility verdict
  • An indicative scale, timeline and budget
2

The Blueprint

The unglamorous stage that decides everything. Governing-body rules read line by line, evidence standards mapped, counting methodology agreed, witnesses and adjudication arranged, permissions identified, a date chosen around exams, festivals and weather.

You leave with
  • Rule pack and evidence checklist
  • Registered attempt and adjudication plan
  • Venue shortlist, permissions map, budget
3

The Gathering

Turning a target number into named, briefed, committed people. Institution outreach, volunteer recruitment and training, partner and sponsor conversations, registration systems, and the communications that make a stranger want to give up their Sunday.

You leave with
  • Registration and participant tracking
  • A trained volunteer and steward corps
  • Partner, sponsor and media commitments
4

The Rehearsal

Site plan, entry and exit flow, marshalling, medical and safety cover, weather contingency, timing rehearsal, a full dry run of the counting method. Anything discovered on the day itself is a failure of this stage.

You leave with
  • Site and safety plan, medical cover
  • Minute-by-minute run sheet
  • A rehearsed count and contingency plan
5

The Moment

Execution. Independent witnesses in position, stewards briefed, professional photography and videography, timestamped and continuous evidence capture, participant records collected exactly as the governing body requires.

You leave with
  • Complete evidence file, built to spec
  • Professional photo and video coverage
  • Witness and participant documentation
6

The Legacy

Submission, adjudication, certification — and then the part most organisers forget. The story, the archive, the thank-yous, and the standing group of people who now know they can do this again.

You leave with
  • Certification and the framed record
  • An archive and a story worth telling
  • A community that survives the event

Questions we are asked
in the first meeting.

How many people do we actually need? +

It depends entirely on the category — some records need eleven people and extraordinary precision, others need several thousand and good marshalling. The honest answer comes out of stage one, not out of a brochure.

What does it cost? +

Costs are driven by scale, venue, adjudication fees and documentation, so we quote after the blueprint rather than before it. We will always tell you which parts of the budget are non-negotiable for the record to stand.

Can you guarantee we will get the record? +

No — and be cautious of anyone who says otherwise. What we can do is make sure the attempt is designed against the actual rules and that the evidence will survive scrutiny, which is where most failed attempts are lost.

Which record bodies do you work with? +

Guinness World Records, Limca Book of Records, Asia Book of Records, India Book of Records, Golden Book of Records and Unique Book of Records, among others. The right body depends on the category and the ambition.

How far in advance should we start? +

Three months is tight but possible for a contained attempt. Anything involving thousands of participants, permissions or a new category deserves six to nine.

Is it safe for children to take part? +

Safety and medical cover are planned at stage four and are non-negotiable. Participation is always voluntary, and we will not run an attempt where a student could be pressured into taking part.

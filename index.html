02Why we gather

We have always known
how to gather.

A fair that fills a desert. A procession that closes a city. A wedding street where nobody quite remembers who was invited, and nobody was ever turned away.

Somewhere along the way, gathering became something reserved for weddings and festivals and very little else. Everything we do is an attempt to give people one more reason — and to make that reason worth the morning it costs them.

05Stories
One morning in January

The four minutes nobody in that stadium has stopped talking about.

They arrived in the dark. Eight thousand of them, in school buses, on scooters, walking in from the main road because the buses could not get closer. Somebody’s mother had made two hundred parathas without being asked.

By six the markers were painted. By seven every one of them was standing on a number they had practised finding for three weeks. Then a woman none of them had ever met counted out loud, and eight thousand people lifted something over their heads and did not move.

Four minutes and eleven seconds. From the ground it looked like nothing at all. It took a drone, and two years, for most of them to understand what they had made.
